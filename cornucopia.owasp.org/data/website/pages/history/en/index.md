#  History of Cornucopia

The following is a recount of events as written down by Colin Watson about the early days. As I enjoy immensely hearing about the history behind OWASP projects, I thought it would be nice to share it here.

## Response to a Training Need

I was working half-time for myself as a web application security consultant and half-time for Blackfoot (a UK risk and infosec consultancy) who had a lot of clients who were subject to the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCIDSS) issued by the PCI Security Standards Council (https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/). PCIDSS requires that developers are trained and apply security knowledge to avoid vulnerabilities in the parts of their systems in-scope for PCIDSS, such as web front ends that take card payments. In summer 2012, I was asked to provide web application security training to a group of developers who were heavily into agile methods. The sessions were arranged for two separate groups, one on a Monday morning and another on the Tuesday morning. Each session was going to be about three hours or so, so I knew there would be a high risk of boredom, fatigue and people switching off during the training. I had seen MS-SDL EoP but struggled to apply it myself to website applications. I decided to create a more web appsec focused version, and with my knowledge of many OWASP projects, homed in on the Secure Programming Practices Quick Reference Guide (SCPQRG) as a source for the attacks. I wanted the game to be closely associated with PCIDSS to suit my upcoming training, and thus put “Ecommerce” in its name. I imagined that other decks might be made for other types of web applications, or other application software, and thus called this one “edition” of many. Recently I had been reading Charles Stross' novel Singularity Sky (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singularity_Sky) which had described “cornucopia machines” with the ability to produce a multitude of “things”. In the same way, the card game would span an abundance of attacks and webapp vulnerabilities, so using the “horn of plenty” concept (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornucopia), I chose the name “Cornucopia”.

MS-SDL EoP was open source and had published source Adobe InDesign files, but I did not have and could not afford an Adobe licence and had never used Adobe software. So in my own time and with a budget of £20, I created a Word document containing the cards laid out in a way they could be printed onto pre-scored/perforated business card blanks - the sort of A4 sheets you can buy from office stationery suppliers. I printed the cards on my own inkjet printer for the training sessions. As a contributor to other OWASP projects, I decided to make it an open source OWASP asset. I had some discussions with Keith Turpin, OWASP SCPQRG from 9 Aug 2012 and published the document called "OWASP Cornucopia - Ecommerce Website Edition" version 0.3 as a file on the OWASP wiki, and sent an email to announce it to the SCPQRG mailing list on 15 August 2012 using the subject “SCP and the draft OWASP Cornucopia” (see Appendix). That announcement also alludes to how Cornucopia might also lead to a(n ecommerce) company's increased prosperity, through good appsec, and presumably a lack of fines and other costs associated with PCIDSS failures.

## From Nothing to Something

Monday 20 August 2012. Despite the self-printed cards, the game was well received during the training. Otherwise there was little interest following my announcement to the single mailing list but in February, as part of keeping up-to-date with legislation and standards which might affect web apps, I found that Cornucopia had been mentioned in a newly updated PCIDSS guidance document: “Information supplement PCI DSS E-commerce Guidelines v2” released on 31 January 2013 (https://listings.pcisecuritystandards.org/pdfs/PCI_DSS_v2_eCommerce_Guidelines.pdf).

I don't know how Cornucopia was identified because it was simply a file referred to by one mailing list email, but if you searched the OWASP wiki for “ecommerce” it was in the few results. I notified the OWASP community about this by sending a message to the SCPQRG mailing list on 1 February 2013  (see Appendix).

That changed everything. I decided that v0.3 needed to be converted into its own OWASP project, mentioning this in another email to the SCPQRG mailing list on 21 February 2013 (see Appendix). I created v1.0 and published it on 25 February 2013 (see history https://wiki.owasp.org/index.php/File:OWASP-Cornucopia-Ecommerce_Website.docx) and submitted a “new project request' to OWASP the same day, with this completed on 5 March. I worked on the wiki (https://wiki.owasp.org/index.php/OWASP_Cornucopia) in April and May 2013, announced to the new Cornucopia mailing list on 18 May 2013. A short presentation about Cornucopia was provided on 3 June 3rd 2013  at OWASP London chapter's event for the OWASP EU Tour 2013. Conference (presentation https://wiki.owasp.org/images/8/8f/Owasplondon-colinwatson-cornucopia.odp).

## Printed decks

OWASP projects had no budgets at the time, and there were no resources to get the game printed or create print-ready design files.

In September 2013 Blackfoot, who I was still working for half-time, offered to print decks of cards to give away free of charge, and donate the design files to OWASP so that others could use them. I insisted Blackfoot's branding did not appear on the cards, and there would be no OWASP branding on Blackfoot's packaging (their card boxes). Blackfoot gave me decks in December 2013 and made them available on request worldwide, to anyone who contacted them. This did cause a lot of grief from a small number of people in OWASP. However, it was the first step in making adoption and use much more widespread, and I was able to give away hundreds of decks at various OWASP chapter meetings, OWASP training and other events.

Eventually Tom Brennan, an OWASP board member, found funding to print card decks in an OWASP branded box in summer 2014, and give them away to attendees at AppSec USA 2014. The remaining stock and subsequent batches were available to order from OWASP for a number of years and were very popular.

## Wiki deck

Darío De Filippis became involved in OWASP Cornucopia in September 2015, eventually becoming a co-leader of the project. Dario conceived and created, and published on 21 January 2016, the "Card Deck Wiki" for the Ecommerce Website Edition, with each card having a unique URL.
